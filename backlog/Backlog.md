- [ ] **SoC base level — lift the night level when the battery will not fill** `[feat]` *(idea of the maintainer, 2026-11-05; prototype first, SE only)*: the battery runs down to SOC_min every night. That leaves no reserve in winter and on days without sun, and it does not help the battery. The optimizer plans within the allowed range, then the level of the SoC curve is raised as far as the headroom allows. On sunny summer days it stays at the bottom. Works together with **MILP look-ahead past SA₂**: with the look-ahead, a small reserve downwards can remain as well.
  - **Limits:** the base level never exceeds 50 %. A reserve below SOC_max stays free for more sun than forecast, sized relative to the battery. Empirical rule, to be derived from yearly runs: `R = clear-sky daily yield / usable capacity`, possibly with the daily load (surplus instead of yield) and other factors.
  - **Raising:** only from PV. Weather-independent fallback: a level that depends on the date, from the clear-sky yield. If the level stays below it for days, a very weak controller may raise it from the grid: integral part only, output tightly limited (for example at most 1 % per day), and its output goes into the MILP as a target energy.
  - **Lowering:** allowed when the next day is expected to yield more.
  - **Measure:** extra cost of a level that is too high before a sunny day against the benefit. The reserve's value against forecast errors only shows with the SE forecast mode ([`se-forecast-mode.md`](../docs/dev/se-forecast-mode.md)); a benefit for battery ageing does not appear in electricity cost and needs a sourced assumption. Calendar ageing at high SoC is the reason for the 50 % limit and should be checked for the battery types.
  - **Tests:** level never above 50 % or into the reserve; no grid charging except the limited controller output; no change on days that fill the battery.
  - **Note (2026-11-05):** first prototype behind `EARNIE_BASE_LEVEL` (needs the look-ahead, here 8 h), `optimizer/milp_horizon_extras.py`: energy held at SA₂ has a value up to a target; the value is below the cheapest buy price, so the target is reached from PV only. Target from placeholder assumptions (first run): day yield 1.5 × look-ahead PV, half of it surplus, 10 % reserve, 50 % cap; since then surplus = PV minus load, with the flex load's real timing in the look-ahead slots. No controller yet, perfect foresight only. Full year on S2–S6 against look-ahead alone: L3 improves or stays level on every scenario (Duo: S2 −41 → −17 €, S5 −20 → −12 €, S4 +9 → +16 €; Dynamisch: S2 +590 → +611 €, S5 +179 → +183 €). Winter night SoC rises by about 2–13 points, summer by 1–5. The rule still has to be derived empirically, and the flex load of the next day is not in it.

  - **MILP:** peak variable `P_peak >= P_import,t` for all slots of the horizon and `P_peak >= peak_so_far`; objective adds the remaining capacity cost from Edibert's ladder (convex piecewise-linear: minimum, band limit 10 kW, higher rate above; two linear segments, no binaries while Edibert reports the bands convex); the cost is once per month, not per slot; a horizon that crosses the month boundary gets a second ladder starting at peak `0`
  - **Planning loop:** the horizon (1 to 2 days) is shorter than the billing month, so the per-slot MILP alone would refuse every exceedance; a slow loop sets a monthly peak budget from Edibert's ladder, `days_remaining` and the expected load (EV, heat pump), and raises the budget after a measured exceedance (the "reached" rungs of the ladder); what-if queries pass a planned peak as state
  - **Expected PV for the rest of the month (discussion 2026-10-14; algorithm draft: [`Capacity-Peak-Budget-Draft.md`](Capacity-Peak-Budget-Draft.md)):** the peak budget needs the PV outlook until month end, but not as a mean: the monthly peak is set by the worst days (dull, cold, heat pump and EV at the same time)
    - Per calendar month, sample real historical days from the Open-Meteo archive (last 3–5 years, house location): daily PV yield and temperature **from the same day** (joint sample, not separate averages; dull winter days are often cold days); use the distribution (e.g. P10/P50), never hour-by-hour averages across years (they smooth away exactly the days that set the peak)
    - Rest of month = forecast days (Open-Meteo forecast; check how many days are reliable) + historical sample days for the remaining days → probability that a peak budget holds until month end
    - Trade-off (ratchet): a higher peak costs one ladder step **once per month**, after that import up to this peak is free for the rest of the month. Compare the step cost with the summed benefit over the remaining days (cheaper import slots for battery/EV, PV sold at a higher feed-in price instead of stored). Early in the month a higher peak pays off more easily, late in the month hardly at all; once the month peak is set, use the headroom up to it actively
    - **SE:** run several real years (weather and prices from the same year) and show the spread of monthly peaks per calendar month (e.g. January 6.8–9.1 kW) instead of one "typical" value; 2022 (energy crisis) must be excludable or prices normalised to a current level
